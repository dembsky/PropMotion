# RealityKit parity research for scenekit-product-stages

Question: how many of the 12 production SceneKit patterns taught by the scenekit-product-stages skill have working RealityKit equivalents on iOS (iPhone/iPad, not visionOS-only), and where are the hard gaps? Purpose: judge feasibility of a companion skill "realitykit-product-stages".

Date: 2026-08-05.

Method: every availability claim was verified against the Apple developer documentation JSON data endpoints (the same data that renders the "iOS 18.0+" availability line on each developer.apple.com page) plus the WWDC24/WWDC25 session pages and Apple's official migration article. No blog or forum claims are load-bearing. Anything not confirmable on a primary source is marked UNVERIFIED.

Scope: iOS/iPadOS product-stage rendering (one polished actor on a transparent stage composited over SwiftUI). visionOS behavior is out of scope except where availability must be disambiguated.

## 1. Transparent stage composited over SwiftUI

RealityView is available on iOS 18.0+, iPadOS 18.0+, macOS 15.0+, tvOS 26.0+, visionOS 1.0+ (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/realityview). On iOS the content type is RealityViewCameraContent (iOS 18.0+, no visionOS availability, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/realityviewcameracontent), which exposes camera, cameraTarget, entities, environment, and renderingEffects. The camera is a RealityViewCamera (iOS 18.0+) with .virtual for a fully non-AR virtual camera ("The camera that displays virtual RealityKit content", https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/realityviewcamera/virtual) and .worldTracking for the AR path; WWDC24 session 10103 shows setting content.camera in an #if os(iOS) block (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10103/). Orbit-style built-in controls come from the .realityViewCameraControls(_:) modifier (iOS 18.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/realityviewcameracontrols(_:)) with the CameraControls options type (iOS 18.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/cameracontrols); a product stage would more likely place its own PerspectiveCameraComponent entity (iOS 13.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/perspectivecameracomponent). The background story is the key nuance: RealityViewCameraContent.environment takes a RealityViewEnvironment (iOS 18.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/realityviewenvironment) whose .default case is documented as "The view uses any background style you apply via the view's background style" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/realityviewenvironment/default), which is Apple's documented hook for compositing RealityKit content over SwiftUI-styled backdrop instead of a skybox. However, the docs never state the word "transparent" and never promise alpha compositing over arbitrary SwiftUI layers the way SCNView.backgroundColor = .clear does; that exact behavior is UNVERIFIED on primary sources and must be validated empirically in the lab app. Sizing is also a real difference: the RealityView page states the view "has a flexible size by default, and does not size itself based on the RealityKit content it displays" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/realityview), so framing an entity means solving camera FOV against entity bounds yourself.

## 2. Shadow catcher and grounding shadow

RealityKit lights were iOS-available long before iOS 18: DirectionalLightComponent, PointLightComponent, and SpotLightComponent are all iOS 13.0+ (visionOS only from 2.0) (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/directionallightcomponent, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/pointlightcomponent, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/spotlightcomponent), and DirectionalLightComponent.Shadow is likewise iOS 13.0+ (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/directionallightcomponent/shadow). So the studio 3-light rig plus dedicated shadow light ports directly, and WWDC25 session 288 shows the two-component Entity(components: DirectionalLightComponent(), DirectionalLightComponent.Shadow()) pattern (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/288/). Per-entity shadow participation is DynamicLightShadowComponent, iOS 18.0+, visionOS 2.0+ ("controls an entity's shadow from dynamic (virtual) lights", https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/dynamiclightshadowcomponent). The cheap path is GroundingShadowComponent, iOS 18.0+, visionOS 1.0+, with castsShadow/receivesShadow and fade behavior near physical objects (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/groundingshadowcomponent); by default it makes an entity receive grounding shadows from other model entities (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/groundingshadowcomponent/init(castsshadow:)). The gap: there is no documented equivalent of SceneKit's .shadowOnly lighting model, that is, an invisible surface that renders ONLY received shadows from your own light rig over a transparent background. OcclusionMaterial (iOS 13.0+) hides geometry behind it and has init(receivesDynamicLighting:) (iOS 13.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/occlusionmaterial/init(receivesdynamiclighting:)), but its docs do not state it composites received shadows on a transparent view background; treat a custom shadow-catcher over transparent SwiftUI as UNVERIFIED and prototype it early, with GroundingShadowComponent as the documented fallback.

## 3. Programmatic environment maps and image-based lighting

The UIGraphicsImageRenderer-painted 2:1 spherical map pattern ports at iOS 18. EnvironmentResource itself is iOS 13.0+ (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/environmentresource), and the runtime generator init(equirectangular:withName:), "Asynchronously generates an environment resource from an equirectangular image" from a CGImage, is iOS 18.0+, macOS 15.0+, visionOS 2.0+ (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/environmentresource/init(equirectangular:withname:)-8o2v7). The older generate(fromEquirectangular:withName:) is deprecated and listed only for visionOS 1.0 (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/environmentresource). Note for the future: the newer init(equirectangular:options:) pair (async and sync) carries an iOS 27.0 beta availability line as of today (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/environmentresource/init(equirectangular:options:)-8e7wv), so do not teach it yet. To apply the map: ImageBasedLightComponent and ImageBasedLightReceiverComponent are both iOS 18.0+, visionOS 1.0+ (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/imagebasedlightcomponent, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/imagebasedlightreceivercomponent), and RealityViewEnvironment.skybox(_:) (iOS 18.0+) can use the same resource as a visible background (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/realityviewenvironment/skybox(_:)). VirtualEnvironmentProbeComponent, "provides environment lighting for entities you place within the same virtual world", is iOS 18.0+, visionOS 2.0+ (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/virtualenvironmentprobecomponent). This chapter is a conceptual straight port: same painted CGImage, different one-call ingestion.

## 4. Baked keyframe choreography and interruption semantics

The baking half ports well. AnimationResource is iOS 13.0+ (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/animationresource). SampledAnimation<Value: AnimatableData>, "cycles through a series of frames at a constant interval", is iOS 15.0+ and is exactly the dense-sampled model the skill teaches: you hand it frames plus frameInterval, with overloads for joint transforms, doubles, and other AnimatableData, plus a dedicated init(weightNames:frames:...) for blend-shape weights (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/sampledanimation). FromToByAnimation (iOS 15.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/fromtobyanimation) with AnimationTimingFunction (iOS 13.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/animationtimingfunction) covers the tween cases, and AnimationGroup (iOS 15.0+) composes simultaneous tracks (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/animationgroup). BindTarget (iOS 15.0+) routes animations to .transform, .jointTransforms, .parameter(_:), and paths; the newer targets .opacity and .material(_:) are iOS 18.0+ and .blendShapeWeights and .billboardBlendFactor are iOS 18.0+/visionOS 2.0+ (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/bindtarget). Playback control: AnimationPlaybackController (iOS 13.0+) has pause/resume/stop, speed, blendFactor (iOS 15.0+), and a settable time ("var time: TimeInterval { get set }", iOS 15.0+), so scrubbing is documented (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/animationplaybackcontroller, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/animationplaybackcontroller/time). stop(blendOutDuration:) is iOS 18.0+ and its docs warn that after stopping "the playback controller becomes invalid" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/animationplaybackcontroller/stop(blendoutduration:)). The hard part is interruption: RealityKit has NO documented equivalent of SceneKit's presentation node. No API is documented for reading the currently-displayed mid-animation value; whether Entity.transform reflects the animated pose during AnimationResource playback is UNVERIFIED on primary sources. The skill's signature "steal a node mid-flight via .presentation snapshot" recipe therefore has no documented port; the closest documented tools are the settable time (compute the pose yourself from your own pure pose function at that time), blendFactor crossfades, and stop(blendOutDuration:) handoffs.

## 5. First-frame pipeline warmup

There is no documented equivalent of SCNView.prepare(_:shouldAbortBlock:). No prewarm, prepare, or shader-precompile symbol exists in the RealityKit documentation tree (a probe of realitykit/entity/prewarm returns 404, and no warmup topic appears under RealityView, MeshResource, or the material types), and neither WWDC24 session 10103 (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10103/) nor WWDC25 session 287 (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/287/) mentions first-draw shader compilation. Apple also does not document whether RealityKit exhibits a first-frame compile hitch at all. Both the problem and any remedy are UNDOCUMENTED; a companion skill can only teach an empirical workaround (for example, showing the content one frame early at zero opacity via OpacityComponent, iOS 18.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/opacitycomponent) and must label it as folklore, not API.

## 6. Particles

ParticleEmitterComponent is iOS 18.0+, macOS 15.0+, tvOS 26.0+, visionOS 1.0+ (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/particleemittercomponent). Capability coverage against SCNParticleSystem is strong: emitter shapes, birth location/direction, bursts with variation, isEmitting, timing, spawned secondary emitters (spawnedEmitter, spawnOccasion), and a mainEmitter ParticleEmitter struct that carries lifetime evolution (color with colorEvolutionPower, opacityCurve, sizeMultiplierAtEndOfLifespan and its power curve), blendMode, sortOrder, billboardMode, noise fields, attraction, vortex, stretchFactor, and image sequences (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/particleemittercomponent/particleemitter). What it lacks is SceneKit's fully generic SCNParticlePropertyController (arbitrary keyframe controllers over any particle property); RealityKit exposes fixed evolution curves per property instead. WWDC25 session 288 confirms there is no converter for .scnp files, "no tool to directly convert these files", so particle presets must be rebuilt by hand or in Reality Composer Pro (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/288/). Warmup-style pre-simulation (SceneKit's warmupDuration) has no documented counterpart; burst() and restart() are the documented controls.

## 7. Custom deformation and materials

CustomMaterial, "a material that works with custom Metal shader functions", is iOS 15.0+ and macOS 12.0+, and its availability line lists NO visionOS at all, so it is exactly an iOS/macOS tool (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/custommaterial). It provides SurfaceShader and GeometryModifier programs, which map one-to-one onto the skill's shader-modifier chapter (surface tinting, vertex wobble). Uniform driving without KVC: CustomMaterial.Custom.value is a SIMD4<Float> "custom vector that the framework sends to the material's shader functions" (iOS 15.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/custommaterial/custom-swift.struct/value), and withMutableUniforms(ofType:_:) gives typed uniform-buffer access at iOS 18.0+ (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/custommaterial/withmutableuniforms(oftype:_:)). ShaderGraphMaterial is NOT visionOS-only: it is iOS 18.0+, macOS 15.0+, visionOS 1.0+, loading MaterialX/Reality Composer Pro graphs, with setParameter(name:value:) at iOS 18.0+ for runtime uniform updates (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/shadergraphmaterial, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/shadergraphmaterial/setparameter(name:value:)). LowLevelMesh and LowLevelTexture (both iOS 18.0+, visionOS 2.0+) cover fully procedural per-frame geometry and texture streaming (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/lowlevelmesh, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/lowleveltexture). For animation-system-driven uniforms, BindTarget.parameter(_:) (iOS 15.0+) and BindTarget.material(_:) paths (iOS 18.0+) replace SceneKit's KVC keypaths (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/bindtarget). One caution for the skill: CustomMaterial does not exist on visionOS, so an iOS-first skill can use it freely but must flag the portability cliff.

## 8. Morph targets and blend shapes

BlendShapeWeightsComponent, "access to the current weights associated with all blend shape meshes on an entity", is iOS 18.0+, macOS 15.0+, visionOS 2.0+ (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/blendshapeweightscomponent), with BlendShapeWeightsMapping at the same availability (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/blendshapeweightsmapping). It is animatable through the baked-animation machinery: BindTarget.blendShapeWeights (iOS 18.0+, "Requires that the entity has a BlendShapeWeightsComponent", https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/bindtarget/blendshapeweights) plus per-index and per-ID variants, and SampledAnimation has a dedicated init(weightNames:frames:...) for per-frame weight arrays (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/sampledanimation). There is no symbol named ShapeMorpher in the RealityKit documentation tree (probe returns 404). Net: the SCNMorpher chapter ports fully at iOS 18, with weights delivered from USD blend shapes rather than geometry-target arrays built in code.

## 9. Gestures and hit-testing

The SwiftUI-native path is fully iOS-available: Gesture.targetedToEntity(_:) is iOS 18.0+, macOS 15.0+, visionOS 1.0+ (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/gesture/targetedtoentity(_:)), delivering an EntityTargetValue (iOS 18.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/entitytargetvalue) that wraps SpatialTapGesture (SwiftUI, iOS 16.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/spatialtapgesture) or DragGesture values together with the hit entity and coordinate conversion. Targets require InputTargetComponent (iOS 18.0+, visionOS 1.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/inputtargetcomponent) plus CollisionComponent (iOS 13.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/collisioncomponent). Manual hit-testing without any AR session is documented since the beginning: Scene.raycast(origin:direction:length:query:mask:relativeTo:) is iOS 13.0+ and casts "against all the geometry in the scene" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/scene/raycast(origin:direction:length:query:mask:relativeto:)). The release-velocity-into-integrator pattern survives: DragGesture supplies predicted/end velocity in view points, and the skill must teach the unproject/convert step through EntityTargetValue or the camera projection to turn points into scene units, since no Apple page documents a one-call velocity conversion (that specific conversion recipe is a derived technique, not cited API). iOS 26 adds GestureComponent for attaching gestures entity-side (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/gesturecomponent).

## 10. Instancing dozens of shared-mesh actors

The iOS 18 story is the same one the SceneKit skill already teaches implicitly: share one MeshResource (iOS 13.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/meshresource) and one material across ~40 ModelComponent entities; Apple documents resource sharing but makes no draw-call-batching promise for it. Real documented GPU instancing arrives with MeshInstancesComponent, which is iOS 26.0+, macOS 26.0+, visionOS 26.0+ across the board ("performs GPU instancing on the model of the same entity", https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/meshinstancescomponent); you provide per-instance transforms in the entity's local space via LowLevelInstanceData. WWDC25 session 287 states the performance claim explicitly: "the MeshInstancesComponent can also improve performance by reducing the amount of data that needs to be sent to the GPU. Instead of sending multiple copies of the model and materials to the GPU when drawing duplicate meshes, the MeshInstancesComponent will only send that data once" (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/287/). Verdict: partial at iOS 18 (shared resources, unknown batching), full at an iOS 26 deployment target.

## 11. Look-at and billboard constraints

BillboardComponent is NOT visionOS-only: it is iOS 18.0+, macOS 15.0+, visionOS 2.0+ and "orients an entity instance so that it continuously points toward the active camera" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/billboardcomponent), with an animatable blend factor via BindTarget.billboardBlendFactor (iOS 18.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/bindtarget/billboardblendfactor). There is no SCNLookAtConstraint equivalent, no constraint system at all in RealityKit. The documented replacements are the one-shot HasTransform.look(at:from:upVector:relativeTo:) (iOS 13.0+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/hastransform/look(at:from:upvector:relativeto:)) called every frame from a custom System (System protocol, iOS 15.0+, "an object that affects multiple entities in every update of a RealityKit scene", https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/system). The camera-rig chapter therefore becomes an ECS chapter: constraints turn into Systems, which is a rewrite of mechanism but a conceptual port of behavior.

## 12. Mirror-floor reflections

SCNFloor, "a plane that can optionally display a reflection of the scene above it", exists only in SceneKit and is deprecated with the rest of the framework as of the 26.0 SDKs (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/scenekit/scnfloor). RealityKit has NO planar reflection or mirror API on any platform: nothing in the materials, components, or rendering documentation renders a live mirrored scene. The only Apple-documented real-time specular reflection feature is the visionOS custom media environment path, the Reflection_Specular shader-graph node driven by a dockingRegion for immersive video environments, shown in WWDC24 session 10115 (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10115/), which is not an iOS API and not a scene mirror. VirtualEnvironmentProbeComponent (iOS 18.0+) gives environment-map reflections, so glossy floors can reflect the static environment, but not the animated product actor. Apple documents no workaround; a render-to-texture mirror via a second camera is not documented for RealityKit on iOS. This chapter has no counterpart.

## Cross-cutting: deprecation, migration guide, WWDC coverage

SceneKit's documentation metadata marks the entire framework deprecated at 26.0 on every platform (iOS 8.0-26.0 deprecated, macOS 10.8-26.0 deprecated, visionOS 1.0-26.0 deprecated) (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/scenekit). The official migration article, "Bringing your SceneKit projects to RealityKit" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/bringing-your-scenekit-projects-to-realitykit), associated with WWDC25 session 288, states the terms plainly: "SceneKit is soft-deprecated... your existing apps and games continue to work... SceneKit is unlikely to gain new capabilities... general bug fixes or performance optimizations are unlikely... migrate to another native framework like RealityKit." It is a sample-driven walkthrough (ECS packaging, character movement, camera management, USD conversion via scntool) rather than an API equivalence table; the API-level mapping lives in WWDC25 session 288 (SCNView/SceneView to RealityView, node to entity plus components, SCN assets to USD, lights via DirectionalLightComponent plus .Shadow, no .scnp particle converter, camera bloom deliberately replaced by custom post-processing through content.renderingEffects) (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/288/). WWDC24 session 10103 is the availability watershed: RealityView, ShaderGraph, particle emitters, portals, lights and shadows and more "now cross-platform" on iOS 18, iPadOS 18, macOS 15 (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10103/). WWDC25 session 287 adds tvOS 26 support, MeshInstancesComponent, GestureComponent, and post-processing effects on iOS (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/287/).

## Hard gaps

- Presentation-node mid-animation reads (pattern 4). No documented API returns the currently-rendered value of an animating property; AnimationPlaybackController exposes time (settable) and blendFactor but not sampled output, and stop(blendOutDuration:) invalidates the controller (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/animationplaybackcontroller). The steal-mid-flight recipe must be re-derived around self-computed poses (the skill's pure pose functions can substitute, since pose(t) at controller.time reproduces the displayed value for self-baked clips).
- Shader/pipeline warmup (pattern 5). No prepare() equivalent, and no Apple documentation of a first-frame compile hitch. Undocumented on both the problem and the cure.
- Planar mirror reflections (pattern 12). No RealityKit equivalent of SCNFloor on iOS or any platform (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/scenekit/scnfloor).
- Shadow-only catcher surface (pattern 2). GroundingShadowComponent gives a system grounding shadow (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/groundingshadowcomponent), but a SceneKit-style invisible plane that shows only your rig's shadows over a transparent background is not documented; feasibility over a transparent RealityView needs lab verification.
- Explicit transparent-background contract (pattern 1). RealityViewEnvironment.default defers to the SwiftUI background style (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/realityviewenvironment/default), but alpha compositing over arbitrary SwiftUI content is nowhere promised in words. Needs one lab experiment before the skill asserts it.
- Not gaps after checking: ShaderGraphMaterial is on iOS 18 (not visionOS-only), BillboardComponent is on iOS 18 (not visionOS-only), CustomMaterial is iOS/macOS-only (absent on visionOS, which helps rather than hurts an iOS skill).

## Verdict

A companion skill "realitykit-product-stages" for iOS is feasible now, with a minimum deployment target of iOS 18.0. That target is forced by the load-bearing cluster: RealityView and RealityViewCameraContent, RealityViewCamera.virtual, GroundingShadowComponent, ParticleEmitterComponent, ImageBasedLightComponent, EnvironmentResource(equirectangular:withName:), targetedToEntity and InputTargetComponent, ShaderGraphMaterial, BlendShapeWeightsComponent, and BillboardComponent are all iOS 18.0. Only a minority of the machinery (lights, raycast, SampledAnimation, CustomMaterial, System) reaches lower.

Conceptual ports (same idea, new spelling): chapter 3 environment maps (same painted CGImage, one-call ingestion), chapter 4 baking (pure pose functions into SampledAnimation frames instead of CAKeyframeAnimation), hand-rolled physics baked to keyframes (unchanged math), chapter 6 particles, chapter 7 shaders (CustomMaterial is arguably nicer than shader-modifier string injection), chapter 8 morphing, chapter 9 gestures and haptics, the 3-light rig.

Full rewrites of mechanism: chapter 1 stage setup (view sizing and entity framing logic differs, transparency contract must be established empirically), chapter 2 shadow catcher (GroundingShadowComponent first, custom catcher experimental), chapter 11 camera rigs (constraints become Systems), chapter 10 swarms (shared resources at iOS 18, MeshInstancesComponent appendix gated on iOS 26), animation interruption (rebuilt on settable time plus blendFactor plus stop(blendOutDuration:), never on a presentation read).

No counterpart, drop or replace: mirror floors (replace with environment-probe gloss or drop), prepare()-style warmup (empirical workaround only, labeled as undocumented), SCNParticlePropertyController-style arbitrary controllers (constrain to RealityKit's fixed evolution curves).
